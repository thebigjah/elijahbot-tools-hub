Plumb

A plumb-line for principled voting. Set the line first. Walk the ballot. Let the line do the deciding.

"Behold, I am setting a plumb line in the midst of my people; I will never again pass them by." Amos VII · 8
Article I

Preparation

Before researching the candidates, confirm your right to vote and obtain a sample ballot. These are universal starting points; they route you to your state's official sites.

On the matter of registration

Use vote.org for a universal check, or usa.gov/voter-registration to register. Most states require registration ~30 days before election day. Same-day registration is available in California, Michigan, Minnesota, and several others. North Dakota requires no registration at all.

Of obtaining the sample ballot

Search "[your state] secretary of state sample ballot" or visit your county election office. Sample ballots are typically published 2–4 weeks before election day, keyed to your address.

Of universal sources for research
  • Ballotpedia — best for state-legislative and federal races; thinner on local.
  • Vote Smart — federal positions, voting records, advocacy-group ratings.
  • Candidate's own site — what they want you to know.
  • Local news search — what's been reported; what controversies exist.
On the conscience of partial research

Do not abstain because you cannot research everything. If your principles are clear, a vote informed by thirty percent of the research is still a vote one hundred percent grounded in your principles. The purpose of this instrument is to stabilize the principles, so that any honest measure of your time yields a values-aligned ballot.

Article II

Of Principles

Declare your principles before meeting the candidates. A principle is a single claim you affirm or oppose, with a measured weight. Stable across elections. Edit when your thinking shifts; do not edit mid-race.
