Before you research candidates, make sure you can vote. These are universal starting points — they route to your own state's official sites.

Are you registered?

Check your registration status
Use vote.org for a universal check, or usa.gov/voter-registration to register. Most states require registration ~30 days before election day; same-day registration available in California, Michigan, Minnesota, and others.

Find your sample ballot

Your state's Secretary of State
Search "[your state] secretary of state sample ballot" or your county election office. States publish your specific ballot 2-4 weeks before election day, keyed to your address.

Universal research sources

Sources Plumb will deep-link to per candidate
  • Ballotpedia — best for state-legislative + federal
  • Vote Smart — federal positions, voting records, ratings
  • Candidate site — what they want you to know
  • Local news search — what's reported, what controversies exist

A word on partial research

Don't skip voting because you can't research everything. If your principles are clear, a 30%-research vote is still a 100%-principled vote. Plumb's job is to stabilize your principles so any amount of research time still produces a values-aligned ballot.

Write your principles before you meet the candidates. A principle is a single claim you agree or disagree with, plus how much it weighs in your decision. Stable across elections. Edit when your thinking changes; don't edit mid-race.

Examples: "Government should provide a safety net for the elderly." "Abortion should be legal in the first trimester." "The death penalty is wrong." Be specific. Vague principles produce vague filtering.
